1 · What the business has actually earned

FormFactor's filed results, run through the same bridge this report uses for the future. Gross margin sat in a 39.0–41.9% band for nine consecutive years, and operating income was no higher in 2025 than in 2017 despite 43% more revenue. Then Q2 FY2026 printed 50.7%. Everything below turns on whether that line is a new floor or a cycle peak.

2 · The bridge

No earnings figure is assumed. Each case names a revenue level, a gross margin and a dollar cost base, and the earnings fall out. That ordering is deliberate: with roughly $290M of largely fixed operating cost against $450–700M of gross profit, a few points of gross margin is the entire valuation, and stating EPS directly would bury the one assumption that matters.

3 · The cases, weighted

Revenue, margin, cost base and multiple move together, so each case varies them jointly. The fair value is the probability-weighted value per share across the cases.

4 · Where the answer actually comes from

Against a largely fixed cost base, value per share is far more sensitive to the gross margin than to anything else in the model. This grid is the honest summary of the report: pick a revenue level and a margin, and read off what the business is worth.

How good are these inputs?

One read per input — what it rests on, and how hard it would be to argue with. The gross margin and the scenario weights are the two that decide this report.

5 · What the price requires

Everything above was computed without looking at the market. Here we run the same bridge in reverse — take the traded price as given, hold the cost base, the tax rate, the share count and the multiple at our figures, and solve for the earnings it implies. Then express that requirement two ways: as a gross margin at our revenue, and as a revenue at our gross margin.

Why the band sits there

The accumulate and trim levels at the top of this page are set from the fair value and from how well this report's own inputs stand up — never from the quote. Weak inputs earn a wider discount before acting, and that mapping is fixed in the engine, so a report cannot award itself a level that happens to clear the price. This is what the figures supported on 2026-08-18; a later report with different figures gets a different band.

Key inputs

Not investment advice. An earnings-power bridge is only as good as the margin assumed at the top of it — with a large fixed cost base, a few points either way moves the answer a long way, which is exactly why the model shows the margin rather than an earnings figure.
