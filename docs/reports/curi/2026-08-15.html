1 · What the parts are

CuriosityStream reports one segment, but it runs three economically different streams, and the 10-Q discloses enough to separate them. A slowly shrinking subscription business. A licensing business that is genuinely growing. And, sitting inside that licensing line, a large and fast-growing block of trade-and-barter revenue — content swapped for content, on which no cash ever changes hands. Averaging those into one multiple would bury the only question that matters.

2 · Building up the value

Each part's expected FY2027 figure times the multiple that stream deserves on its own merits. The barter block is carried at nil in the bear and base cases: it is real revenue under GAAP, it is not cash, and it cannot pay a dividend.

3 · The same build-up, three ways

The parts move together, so each case varies the figures and the multiples jointly. The fair value is the probability-weighted per-share value across the cases.

4 · What the price leaves over

Everything above was computed without looking at the market. Here we do the reverse — take the traded price as given, credit every other part and the cash at our figures, and read what is left over for the one part that decides the answer.

Why the band sits there

The accumulate and trim levels at the top of this page are set from the fair value and from how well this report's own inputs stand up — never from the quote. Weak inputs earn a wider discount before acting, and that mapping is fixed in the engine, so a report cannot award itself a level that happens to clear the price. This is what the figures supported on 2026-08-15; a later report with different figures gets a different band.
